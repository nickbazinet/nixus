Light-mode directions · marketing site · comparison artifact

Three light-mode directions for the Nixus marketing site

This is a comparison artifact, not production UI. Nothing here is wired to apps/web, no file in the app was touched, and DESIGN.md remains authoritative until a direction is chosen and the spine is amended to match it. No winner is implied — the three tabs are peers.

Each tab renders the same representative page slice: the site header, the hero, the three value pillars, the AI statement-import demo, and six of the eight feature cards. Copy, section order, grid, and component anatomy are identical across all three, so the only variable is visual treatment — surface, depth, boundary and radius.

The problem being tested. Live light mode repeats cool pale blue/lavender surfaces across large sections, sets low-depth white cards on top of them, and centres nearly every composition. The result reads washed out: the cards barely separate from their band, and the page has no tonal rhythm to break the scroll.

All three directions treat that as a surface and boundary problem rather than a text-contrast problem. They differ in which device does the separating work — tone (A), a stronger hairline (B), or one controlled elevation plus atmosphere (C).

  • Shared by all three: the Nixus logo gradient on the mark and wordmark; the periwinkle brand family reserved for brand and action only; status colours untouched; no shadow on any hairline-separated element in A and B.
  • Money is set in the UI face with tabular figures, never a code font — including the raw statement column, which gets its export look from uppercase and letterspacing instead.
  • Type is the system UI stack because this file makes no network requests; production bundles Inter locally. Judge palette, depth and boundaries here — not letterforms.
  • Light only. No dark mode is proposed or shown.
  • The slice is a static rendering. Nothing inside a frame is focusable, so keyboard-testing the tabs is not polluted by mock controls.

Use ← / → on the tabs to flip between directions in place — the fastest way to see a palette difference is to switch without the page moving. The Phone width toggle constrains each frame to 390px; the slice reflows from its own container, so the toggle and a real 390px browser window produce the same layout.

Direction A

Warm & Calm — closest to the shipped Quiet Ledger spine

Zero new tokens. Warm paper #FAF8F5 instead of cool pale blue, pure-white cards one tonal step above it, hairline-only separation, shadow: none, and the crisp 5/7px radii. The washed-out reading is repaired by making every band warm and alternating paper against chrome, so a white card always has something to sit on.

  • page #FAF8F5
  • band #F5F2EC
  • card #FFFFFF
  • line #E8E3DA
  • brand #5B54D6
  • ink #1C1917

What changes

  • Cool pale blue/lavender bands become bg paper and chrome, alternating section to section — that alternation is the only new rhythm device.
  • Cards are pure white on paper, so the boundary is a real luminance step rather than a faint ring.
  • Eyebrows sit in brand-ink with no rule; the brand is spent on the one primary action and the active destination underline.
  • Radii, hairlines, and the no-shadow baseline are exactly as the spine already specifies.

Trade-offs

  • Lowest risk and no spine amendment — but the least visually distinctive of the three, and the least likely to read as “premium” to a first-time visitor.
  • All depth rests on one bg ↔ card luminance step. That step is small, and hairlines measure 1.2–1.6:1, so a low-vision visitor gets little help from either.
  • Band alternation is the only device breaking a long scroll; below the fold the marketing page can still feel flat.
  • Warm paper is the correct register but it is quiet — this direction wins on calm and loses on impact.

Direction B

Crisp & Modern — brighter base, tighter boundaries

A white page with warm-neutral bands, and separation moved off tone onto the hairline: line-strong becomes the default card border, with a 2px top edge that makes each card read as a drawn object. Corners tighten to 4/5px, brand deepens to #4A43BE for higher-contrast type, and each eyebrow gets a short brand rule.

  • page #FFFFFF
  • band #F7F5F1
  • card #FFFFFF
  • line-strong #CBC3B5
  • brand #4A43BE
  • ink #16130F

What changes

  • Page surface goes to #FFFFFF; the warmth survives only in the bands and the hairlines, which is what makes it read brighter and more precise.
  • Card borders promote from line to line-strong, with a 2px top edge — boundaries you can see at arm's length rather than infer.
  • Radii tighten one step across the board; icon tiles lose their fill and become outlined, so the brand tint stops repeating.
  • Deeper brand ink lifts eyebrow, link and destination contrast, and the eyebrow rule gives each section a fixed left anchor.

Trade-offs

  • Highest legibility of the three and the most software-grade precision — but it reads closer to a well-built devtool than to a well-set financial statement, which is the register the spine picked deliberately.
  • Promoting line-strong to the default card border is a spine change, not a re-theme: {components.card} currently specifies line.
  • On a white page a white card has no tonal step; the border is doing all the work, so the card boundary now depends entirely on a device the spine calls “reinforcement”.
  • Losing paper on the page surface removes the single largest carrier of the calm register. The outlined tiles also make the page quieter in colour, which can read as under-designed.

Direction C

Rich & Premium — atmosphere and one controlled elevation

A deeper warm paper #F3EEE5 so white cards have somewhere to rise from, a single soft warm shadow on the two containers that earn it, a low-alpha wash of the Nixus logo gradient behind the hero only, and gradient eyebrow rules. Radii step up one notch to 6/9px. The intent is depth without noise — still flat prose, still calm.

  • page #F3EEE5
  • band #EDE6DA
  • card #FFFFFF
  • line #E1D9CA
  • brand #524BCB
  • hero wash logo gradient @ 12–20%

What changes

  • The page surface drops two steps to a fuller paper, which widens the bg ↔ card luminance step that A depends on and B gives up.
  • One controlled elevation, scoped to the feature card and the demo window. Pillars stay flat, so the page has a single depth step instead of a field of floating boxes.
  • Three soft washes of the logo gradient — periwinkle, violet, pink — sit behind the hero at 12–20% alpha and fade to paper before the fold. Warm-weighted, no hard edge, no band repeat.
  • Eyebrow rules carry the logo gradient; the primary button gains a 1px seat shadow so it reads as pressable rather than painted.

Trade-offs

  • The strongest answer to “washed out” — and the most expensive: it spends two spine rules, shadow: none and “no gradients on surfaces”. Adopting it means amending DESIGN.md, not re-theming it.
  • Once one card is allowed a shadow, “the elevation recipe lives inside the component” has to be re-enforced or 55 call sites will re-add shadow-sm everywhere.
  • The wash needs a forced-colors fallback and a re-run of ink-faint's four-surface contrast check, because the hero is now a fifth surface.
  • Deeper paper narrows the headroom under warm ink; every text pair needs re-measuring, and the direction risks reading decorative if the alpha ever creeps up.
