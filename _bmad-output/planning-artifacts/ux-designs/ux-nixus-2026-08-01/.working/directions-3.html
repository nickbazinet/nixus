Nixus — three design directions

Same screen, same real content, three complete visual personalities. Every direction keeps the Nixus logo and derives its accent from the logo's own gradient rather than the default indigo it currently ships.

Light and dark are shown for each, because defaultTheme="system" means both modes ship to every user — a direction that only works in one mode isn't a direction.

⚠ Historical artifact. Rendered before the computational contrast pass. The chart ramp, ink-faint, and micro-label sizes here were superseded — the light ramp originally walked the logo gradient smoothly, which gave adjacent segments near-identical luminance (as low as 1.00:1) and reproduced the very problem it was meant to fix. DESIGN.md carries the corrected alternating-luminance ramp. The spine wins on conflict; read token values from DESIGN.md, never from this file. Kept as the record of why Direction A was chosen.
What you're looking at: the Finance dashboard rebuilt to answer three questions instead of showing 29 numbers — Can I spend? · What needs attention? · What should I do? — plus the promoted four-destination nav, an 8-step chart palette with direct labels, and one state variant per direction. Pick a direction and it gets locked into DESIGN.md as the token layer.
Direction A

Quiet Ledger

Warm paper instead of cold slate. Separation comes from hairlines and tone, never shadows — the visual language of a well-set financial statement rather than a dashboard. Numbers are the loudest thing on screen and nothing competes with them.

Register: calm, credible, quiet. Reads as trustworthy institution. Best fit for Marie's "in control, relieved, confident" — and the furthest from what you ship today.

Direction B

Soft Focus

The logo's own palette, taken seriously. Periwinkle through violet through pink becomes the chart ramp; neutrals pick up a violet cast so the brand feels native rather than applied. Soft elevation and 14px radii make it the friendliest of the three.

Register: warm, approachable, consumer-grade. The only direction that looks like your logo. Watch: pink is decorative only — never a status — so it can't be mistaken for over-budget.

Direction C

Clear Slate

The conservative repair. Keeps your slate family and #4F46E5 for actions, and changes only what's provably broken: the page background now sits darker than cards so surfaces actually layer, status colours are split out of the brand and chart families, and a real type scale replaces the 12/14px wall.
