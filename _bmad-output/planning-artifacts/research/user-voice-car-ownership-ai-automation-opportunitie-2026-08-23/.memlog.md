- (decision) Approved standard breadth-first plan: user pain/workarounds, affluent-owner advantages, current solutions/gaps, then feature scoring; 3 researchers, up to 8 sources per dimension, depth 2, normal validation.
- (source) Round 1 owner-pain batch: 9 claims across Ducker Carlisle, AARP, NHTSA, J.D. Power, UK FOS, CDK, CFPB summary, AAA, and Capital One; strongest signals are repair opacity, recall follow-through, insurance comprehension, add-on complexity, and breakdown prevention.
- (source) Round 1 affluent-advantages batch: 10 claims across insurer concierge, claims advocacy, buying concierge, estimate review, pickup/delivery, and resale; expert interpretation and coordination are more software-reproducible than physical or licensed services.
- (source) Round 1 competitive batch: 11 claims across CARFAX, FIXD, RepairPal, Jerry, CarAdvise, Openbay, Wrench, CoPilot, NHTSA, and Transport Canada; incumbents specialize by lane and recurring trust failures concern billing and quote transparency.
- (assumption) Project-fit scoring treats existing maintenance history, schedules, alerts, and read-only AI queries as constraints rather than research evidence; the next feature must add decision support rather than duplicate tracking.
- (source) Round 2 verification batch: repair opacity persists, premium repair oversight exists, paid estimate review is a real category, and automated estimate analysis is demonstrated but accuracy-limited; exact repair savings, prevalence, and diagnostic accuracy remain unverified.
- (source) Round 2 Canada batch: Ontario, Quebec, Alberta, and Manitoba have materially different repair estimate/authorization rules; Ontario has the strongest current primary-source checklist basis; national generalization is unsafe.
