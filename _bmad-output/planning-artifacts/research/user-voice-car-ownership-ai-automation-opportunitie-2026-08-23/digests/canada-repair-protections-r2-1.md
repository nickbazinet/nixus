# Canada Repair Protections Digest

Accessed: 2026-08-23. This is factual landscape research, not legal advice. Rules are provincial and must not be generalized nationally.

1. **Ontario requires a written estimate unless the consumer declines and sets a maximum, and caps the invoice at 10% above the estimate.** Sources: https://www.ontario.ca/page/car-repair-shops-your-rights and https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/02c30/v37; publisher: Government of Ontario; guidance updated 2025-04-01; confidence: high; class: statutory rule; jurisdiction: Ontario.
2. **Ontario regulates estimate and invoice contents**, including vehicle/odometer identification, itemized parts and condition, labour method, other charges, totals, dates, invoice warranty terms, and statutory-rights text. Source: https://www.ontario.ca/page/guide-auto-repair-businesses; publisher: Government of Ontario; updated 2025-08-19; confidence: high; class: regulation/guidance; jurisdiction: Ontario.
3. **Ontario requires authorization for charged work, with non-written authorization recorded by the repairer.** Sources: https://www.ontario.ca/page/car-repair-shops-your-rights, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/02c30/v37, and https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/050017; publisher: Government of Ontario; current; confidence: high; class: statutory rule; jurisdiction: Ontario.
4. **Ontario provides a minimum 90-day/5,000-km warranty on covered installed parts and labour, with exemptions.** Source: https://www.ontario.ca/page/guide-auto-repair-businesses; publisher: Government of Ontario; updated 2025-08-19; confidence: high; class: statutory rule; jurisdiction: Ontario.
5. **Ontario's Consumer Protection Act, 2023 is not yet in force; the 2002 Act remains operative as of 2026-08-23.** Sources: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/23c23 and https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/02c30/v37; publisher: e-Laws; currency dates 2026-08-18/07; confidence: high; class: time-sensitive statutory status.
6. **Quebec differs materially:** written evaluation is mandatory above $100; an accepted evaluation binds the merchant absent new authorization; minimum warranty is three months/5,000 km for covered repairs over $50. Sources: https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/commercant/secteur/vehicule/garage/reparation/evaluation, https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/version/lc/P-40.1?code=se%3A168, and https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/consommateur/bien-service/vehicule/reparation/conseils; publisher: Quebec OPC/LégisQuébec; 2025/current; confidence: high; class: statutory rule; jurisdiction: Quebec.
7. **Alberta and Manitoba cap overages at 10% up to $100, unlike Ontario's pure 10%; current British Columbia rules were not adequately verified.** Sources: https://www.amvic.org/repair-estimates-and-authorizations-what-you-need-to-know/, https://www.alberta.ca/consumer-bill-of-rights, and https://gov.mb.ca/cca/MobilePages/cpo/car_repair.html; publishers: AMVIC/Governments of Alberta and Manitoba; current/2025; confidence: high for AB/MB; class: provincial rule. BC source https://www.consumerprotectionbc.ca/2012/03/automobile-repairs/ is too stale for operational reliance; status unverified.
8. **Canada has no single federal auto-repair estimate regime; federal consumer guidance routes users to provincial sources.** Source: https://ised-isde.canada.ca/office-consumer-affairs-consumer-hub/en/sector/term/97; publisher: ISED Office of Consumer Affairs; accessed 2026-08-23; confidence: medium-high; class: jurisdictional structure.

## Product Constraints

- Legal-rights checks must be province-gated and source-dated.
- Ontario provides the strongest evidence for a narrow first release.
- The eventual proclamation of Ontario's 2023 Act is a mandatory refresh trigger.
- Second opinions are guidance, not a statutory right in the jurisdictions checked.
- Saskatchewan, Atlantic provinces, territories, Alberta warranty details, and current BC status remain unverified.
