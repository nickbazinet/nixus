# PRD Quality Review — AI-Generated Home Dashboard

## Overall verdict

This is a genuinely well-built PRD with a real thesis and almost no theater: the Vision names a specific tension instead of a platitude, prior art is credited rather than dressed up as novelty, every one of FR-1…FR-10 carries a "Consequences (testable)" block, and the counter-metrics are real anti-goals ("a cluttered, AI-maximized dashboard is a failure of taste, not a win"). What's at risk is almost entirely *outside* the document's own walls — three of its brownfield premises don't survive contact with the codebase, most seriously the addendum's merging of `ai/chat.rs`'s read-only `tool_call` fence with `commands/chat.rs`'s separate `ACTION_TYPES` write set, and the six-component catalog is costed as reuse when `PieChart` doesn't exist anywhere in the app and the three existing charts are bespoke domain components, not generic primitives.

Internally this is close to ready for `bmad-architecture`: traceability closes in both directions, the Glossary holds, the Assumptions Index round-trips. But the external references need a correction pass first, or the architecture phase starts from a wrong map of the code — and the Success Metrics need either an instrumentation story or honest demotion, because a local-first pre-alpha with no telemetry cannot measure any of SM-1 through SM-3 as written.

---

## Decision-readiness — adequate

The PRD makes decisions and mostly says so. §6.2's `[NOTE FOR PM]` is placed at a real tension, not a safe checkpoint — "this is the single highest-risk UX bet in this PRD; if early use shows users fighting the AI to get small layout tweaks right, this should be the first thing reconsidered" is exactly the kind of callout the rubric is looking for, and it names the falsifying observation rather than just flagging unease. The addendum's "Options considered" does the harder work: the rejected "AI sees real data" option is credited with its actual benefit ("pick a log scale, or decide a metric is 'trending down' and color it accordingly") before being rejected, which is what naming a trade-off means. §5's deferral of bi-directional widgets likewise admits the prior art *has* the capability and Nixus is declining it, rather than quietly omitting it.

Where it softens is the Open Questions section, which is half rhetorical. §8 #4 asks whether Metric/Table widgets need empty states and answers itself in the same sentence — "likely yes, left to UX spec rather than this PRD." §8 #1 asks for the retry bound but §9 already fixes it at 1 and FR-5's consequence pre-closes the debate — "exact count is a build-time decision, not re-litigated here." Two of four Open Questions are therefore closed items wearing an open label, which inflates the appearance of deliberation while reducing the section's real signal.

The more consequential gap is that the PRD's headline differentiator is asserted without its residual exposure. §1 claims "the AI needs to see the user's *request* to compose a dashboard, but it does not need to see the user's *numbers* to do it," and §2.1 turns that into a JTBD ("assurance that building my custom dashboard doesn't mean shipping my actual balances and transactions off my machine"). That's true of the app's resolved data — but the user's own prompt is free text and can carry the numbers directly ("why did I spend $4,200 on dining in March"), and the AI-authored widget titles the addendum sketches (`"title": "Net Worth, Last 6 Months"`) are free strings the model writes. The claim as phrased is defensible; the claim as a *user-facing assurance* has an acknowledged-nowhere hole. A reader pushing back on the privacy pitch would find their objection undodged only because it was never raised.

### Findings

- **high** Privacy differentiator asserted without its residual exposure (§1 ¶3, §2.1 bullet 4, §4.2 Feature-specific NFRs) — The NFR constrains what *the app* puts in the prompt ("only Data Source *names, descriptions, and parameter shapes*"), but the Dashboard Composer's whole input is user free text, which is unconstrained and can contain balances, amounts, and merchant names. §2.1 promises "assurance that building my custom dashboard doesn't mean shipping my actual balances and transactions off my machine," which the design does not deliver for user-typed content. *Fix:* add a paragraph in §1 or a `[NOTE FOR PM]` at §4.2 stating that the guarantee covers app-supplied context only, that the user's own prompt text is transmitted verbatim like any other AI feature, and that AI-generated widget titles are free text — then reconcile the §2.1 JTBD wording to match.
- **medium** Two of four Open Questions are pre-answered (§8 #1, §8 #4) — #1 is resolved by §9 and foreclosed by FR-5's "not re-litigated here"; #4 answers itself with "likely yes." *Fix:* move #1 to §9/§4.3 as a settled decision with its rationale, restate #4 as a UX-spec handoff item rather than a question, and keep §8 for the two genuinely live ones (#2 sync merge semantics, #3 reset/rebuild).
- **medium** One `[NOTE FOR PM]` for a feature with several live tensions (§6.2 only) — The layout-editing bet is flagged; the launch-destination question (see Scope honesty), the "Home" vs. existing "Today" naming overlap, and the metrics-without-telemetry problem are not. *Fix:* add callouts at §4.5 (route/launch behavior) and §7 (how any SM gets measured on a local-first app).

## Substance over theater — strong

This is the dimension the PRD is best on, and it earns it mostly by subtraction. There are **no personas** — §2.1 uses four JTBD statements instead, each of which maps to a shipped requirement (describe-a-view → FR-4; still-there-next-time → FR-8; refine-by-talking → FR-6; privacy assurance → §4.2 NFR). For a single-user, single-profile, solo-built app, inventing personas would have been pure furniture, and the PRD declined.

The Vision passes the swap test cleanly. "An LLM that can build 'whatever the user likes' is normally either boring (a fixed menu of pre-built widgets) or dangerous (the model emits live code that runs with the app's own database access)" names a specific failure dichotomy and a specific resolution; it could not be lifted into another PRD without rewriting. Equally notable is the absence of innovation theater — §1 explicitly says Nixus "resolves this the way Google's A2UI protocol and CopilotKit's custom-catalog pattern do," and the addendum names the licence, the package (`@copilotkit/a2ui-renderer`), and the blog post. A PRD that could have claimed a novel mechanism instead cites its sources and scopes down to "it borrows the *separation of structure from data* and the *closed catalog* idea, not the wire protocol."

NFR theater is absent too, which is rare. There are exactly two feature-specific NFRs in the whole document, both product-anchored and neither boilerplate: §4.2's prompt-content constraint and §4.3's "routes through the existing `ai/backend.rs` port." Nothing anywhere says the system must be scalable, secure, or reliable. The counter-metrics are the strongest tell that this content is earned rather than assembled — SM-C2's "rising retry usage over time signals catalog/prompt drift, not normal operation" is a real operational insight, not a template slot.

### Findings

- **low** The two normative NFRs are the only requirements in the document with no Consequences block (§4.2, §4.3 Feature-specific NFRs) — Every FR-n has one; the two NFRs do not, including the privacy constraint that §1 elevates to a differentiator. The result is that the most strategically important statement in the PRD is the least testable one. *Fix:* give §4.2's NFR a consequence an engineer can assert against — e.g. "the outbound generation payload contains no string or numeric value sourced from the local database" — which is directly testable against a seeded profile.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

There is a thesis, it is stated in one sentence, and the features are visibly derived from it. §1: "The model gets creative freedom over structure and composition; it never gets a code path into the user's data." FR-1/FR-2 are the bounded freedom, FR-3 plus its assumption is the sealed data path, FR-4/FR-5 are the enforcement, and §5's first two Non-Goals are the same thesis restated as a perimeter ("Not a general-purpose UI builder," "Not code generation"). Prioritization follows from the thesis rather than from ease: the PRD spends all of its novelty budget on the composition surface and deliberately freezes the data layer to domains that already exist, which is the correct shape for an **experience**-kind MVP and matches §6's scope logic.

Metrics are where coherence thins. SM-2 is a genuinely good thesis validator — first-attempt Surface Validation pass rate measures precisely whether the closed-catalog bet works in practice, and it is the number that would tell you to abandon or expand the approach. SM-1, by contrast, is near-tautological: "% of Home page visits (after first use) that show a previously-generated Home Surface without needing a new AI call — target ≥ 80%." If FR-8 works at all this is ~100%; if it's at 79% the feature is broken, not underperforming. It measures that saving saves. SM-3 is the closest to activity measurement, and the PRD knows it — SM-C1 is explicitly placed to counterbalance it, which is more discipline than most PRDs show.

The sharper coherence gap: the thesis has two halves, and only one is measured. Privacy is elevated in §1 ("This is also a privacy win specific to Nixus's existing stance"), in §2.1, and in the §4.2 NFR — but no SM validates it, no FR consequence asserts it, and nothing in §6.1 scopes the work to verify it. For a feature whose differentiator is "the structure round-trips through the AI, the numbers don't," that claim is the single most verifiable thing in the document and the only thesis element with no validation attached anywhere.

### Findings

- **medium** SM-1 is a smoke test presented as a primary success metric (§7 Primary) — At ≥80% it cannot distinguish success from failure, only working-from-broken; it validates FR-8's existence, not the product bet. *Fix:* demote to a release gate/regression assertion, and replace the primary slot with something that tests whether asked-for dashboards are *kept* — e.g. share of generated Widgets surviving 7+ days without removal, which actually tests whether the AI composes things users want.
- **medium** The thesis's privacy half is unmeasured and unscoped (§7 vs. §1 ¶3, §4.2 NFR) — No SM, no FR consequence, and no §6.1 scope line covers verifying that no local financial values appear in the outbound payload. *Fix:* add an SM or an explicit acceptance assertion for payload content, and add a §6.1 scope line for the test that enforces it.
- **medium** Metric targets have no derivation (§7 SM-1 "≥ 80%", SM-2 "≥ 85%") — Neither names a baseline, a comparable, or the reasoning behind the threshold, so neither can be argued with or recalibrated. *Fix:* state the basis (prior-art observation, internal dogfooding, or explicitly "placeholder pending first telemetry") so downstream knows which numbers are claims and which are guesses.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

The FR-level discipline here is above average and genuinely usable. Every FR has a "Consequences (testable)" block, and most consequences are binary conditions an engineer can turn directly into a test. FR-1's "A generated Home Surface referencing any Component name outside this set fails Surface Validation (FR-4) and never renders" is assertable. So are FR-2's "A Layout Primitive is rejected by Surface Validation if it is bound to a Data Source," FR-6's "A follow-up request that only adds a Widget leaves every other existing Widget on the Home Surface unchanged," and FR-8's "Each Widget's displayed values reflect live data at the time Home is opened, not the data available when the Widget was generated." There is not a single "handles X gracefully" sitting in a consequences block — even FR-7, which is *titled* "Graceful degradation," resolves to concrete behavior and anchors itself to a real code construct (`AppError::HostedAi`, confirmed to carry `recoverable: bool` in `src-tauri/src/error.rs` with a mirrored `hostedAiIsRetryable` in `src/lib/appError.ts`). That consequence is verifiable today.

Three gaps will be hit by story creation immediately, and they are the expensive kind.

First, there are no bounds of any kind on time or size — nowhere in the document. FR-4 describes an AI round-trip with up to two generation attempts and gives it no latency budget. FR-7 and FR-8 describe opening Home and resolving up to seven live Data Sources against SQLite and give that no budget either. Nothing caps Widget count per Home Surface (SM-C1 declines to *chase* widget count upward but no requirement bounds it), and nothing caps the size of the Home Surface description going to or coming back from the model. An engineer cannot tell whether an 8-second Home open is a pass.

Second, FR-6 is titled "Conversational add/modify/remove" and §6.1 scopes "add/modify/remove," but the consequences cover only *add* and *remove*. There is no testable consequence for **modify** — and modify is by far the hardest of the three: does "make that a bar chart instead" preserve the Widget's id, its title, its position in the Layout, its Data Binding params? Is it a patch or a regenerate-and-replace? The one operation most needing acceptance criteria has none, in a PRD that otherwise supplies them everywhere.

Third, the referent-resolution problem is visible in the PRD and unaddressed by it. UJ-3 has the user say "remove the spending categories one" — matching loose natural language onto a specific persisted Widget is the actual hard engineering problem in FR-6, and there is no consequence covering ambiguity (two plausibly-matching Widgets), no-match, or a wrong-match that destroys the wrong Widget. UJ-4 covers malformed *generation*; it does not cover correctly-formed but mis-*targeted* edits, which is a different failure with worse consequences since FR-6 persists "immediately" (§2.3 UJ-3) and there is no undo anywhere in the document.

### Findings

- **high** No latency, size, or count bounds anywhere in the document (§4.3, §4.4, FR-4, FR-7, FR-8) — Generation time, Home-open time with N Data Sources resolving, max Widgets per Home Surface, and max Home Surface description size are all unspecified, so "done" is unfalsifiable on performance for a feature whose core loop is an AI round-trip plus N live queries. *Fix:* add a Feature-specific NFR to §4.3 with a generation budget (including the retry) and to §4.4 with a Home-open budget, plus a hard Widget cap enforced by Surface Validation — the cap also gives SM-C1 something to bite on.
- **high** FR-6's "modify" has no testable consequence (§4.3 FR-6) — Both consequences cover add and remove; modify, the hardest case, is unspecified as to identity preservation, layout position, partial-vs-full replacement, and whether it is implemented as remove+add. *Fix:* add a consequence stating what modify preserves (e.g. "a modify request retains the Widget's id and position in the Layout and changes only the properties named in the request") so downstream can design the patch semantics rather than guess.
- **medium** Widget referent resolution is unspecified despite being surfaced in a UJ (§2.3 UJ-3, §4.3 FR-6) — "remove the spending categories one" requires matching free text to a persisted Widget, with no stated behavior for ambiguous matches, no matches, or confirmation before a destructive edit; §2.3 says "Each change is persisted immediately" and no undo exists. *Fix:* add a consequence for the ambiguous/no-match path, and reconsider whether removal should reuse the existing confirm-then-persist chat-action pattern rather than applying immediately.
- **low** FR-9 implies an unnamed registry addition (§4.4 FR-9) — The claim "the same shape as existing domain audit logging" is accurate (`db/audit.rs`'s `audit::record` takes `old_value`/`new_value` into the `audit_log` table from migration `006`), but the existing `AUDITED_TABLES` map is a closed list of 11 entity types that does not include Home Surface. *Fix:* note that enrolling the Home Surface in `AUDITED_TABLES` is part of FR-9's work, so it doesn't read as free.

## Scope honesty — adequate

§5 Non-Goals does real work rather than padding. Six entries, each naming something a reader could otherwise silently assume, and two of them double as the thesis perimeter. The deferrals are honest about cost: "the bi-directional pattern A2UI supports is explicitly deferred, not adopted now" states that the borrowed prior art includes a capability Nixus is choosing to leave on the table, which is the opposite of a quiet omission. §2.2 Non-Users adds a second layer, §6.2 a third with v2 candidacy labels, and §6.2's layout-editing entry proposes the de-scope with its reconsideration trigger attached rather than burying it.

The four `[ASSUMPTION]` tags are all substantive inferences rather than decoration, and all four round-trip correctly into §9. Open-items density — 4 Open Questions + 4 assumptions + 1 `[NOTE FOR PM]` = 9 — is proportionate here. Against the nominal "launch-grade" calibration that's arguably light; against the actual product (pre-alpha, solo-built, `status: draft`) it reads right.

Two omissions are doing damage, though, and both are the silent-inference kind this dimension exists to catch.

The first is the launch destination. §4.5 places Home "above the existing Finance, Car, and AI items" and calls it "additive to (not a replacement of) the existing static dashboard at `/`." Verified against the codebase: `AppSidebar.tsx` has exactly three rail items in that order, and `/` is simultaneously the app's launch route *and* the Finance rail's default destination, labelled `nav.today` → **"Today"**. So adding a surface named "Home" in the first nav position raises two questions the PRD never answers — does the app now open on Home, and what route does Home occupy if Finance keeps `/`? FR-10's consequences assert "The existing Finance static dashboard remains reachable at its current location" and "Opening 'Home' never requires passing through Finance first," both of which read as settled while leaving the actual IA decision unmade. A reader can walk away believing either answer.

The second is a direct contradiction between the PRD and its own addendum. §4.2 FR-3's assumption states the strong rule: "if the AI output ever contains a literal financial figure, Surface Validation treats it as not a valid Data Binding and rejects the Widget." But the addendum's illustrative schema includes `"title": "Net Worth, Last 6 Months"` — a free-text field the model authors. A free-text title can contain a figure ("Dining: $4,200 in March"), so the rule as written either rejects legitimate output or is unimplementable without first partitioning the schema into constrained and free-text fields. The addendum's own note — "Note what's absent: no dollar figures" — holds for `dataBinding` but not for `title`.

### Findings

- **high** Whether Home becomes the app's launch destination is unstated (§4.5, FR-10) — Verified: `/` is currently both the launch route and the Finance rail's default destination (labelled "Today"). The PRD places Home first in the nav without saying whether it takes over launch, and without assigning Home a route. Both readings are available to a reader, and the two are materially different products. *Fix:* state the decision explicitly in §4.5 with a `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` or `[NOTE FOR PM]` — either "Home becomes the launch destination; `/` moves to `/today`" or "Home is opt-in navigation only; launch behavior is unchanged" — and name Home's route.
- **high** The literal-figure rejection rule contradicts the addendum's free-text `title` field (§4.2 FR-3 `[ASSUMPTION]`, §9 entry 2, vs. addendum "Sketch") — A blanket "any literal financial figure is invalid" rule cannot coexist with an AI-authored `title` string; the rule needs a field scope. *Fix:* restate the assumption as field-scoped — literal values prohibited in `dataBinding` and any data-carrying property, with `title` explicitly allowed as free text — and note in §1/§4.2 that AI-authored titles are a (small) outbound-and-returning text channel.
- **medium** Forward compatibility of persisted Home Surfaces is unaddressed (§5 bullet 1, §6.2 bullet 5, FR-8) — §5 says expanding the Catalog/Whitelist is "a deliberate, future app change," but nothing covers what happens to an already-persisted Widget when a Component or Data Source is *renamed or removed* in a later version. Since FR-8 persists AI-composed structure indefinitely and Nixus ships auto-updates, this is a live migration concern, not a hypothetical. *Fix:* add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` or Open Question on catalog versioning and the behavior for orphaned Widgets on load (drop, placeholder, or re-ask).

## Downstream usability — adequate

This PRD declares itself chain-top in §0 — written "for the downstream BMAD workflows (`bmad-architecture`, `bmad-create-epics-and-stories`) that will turn it into a build plan" — so this dimension applies at full weight. Internally, it is close to exemplary. §3's nine Glossary terms are used verbatim and with consistent capitalization across §4, §5, §6, §7, §8, and §9, with no drift in case, plural, or synonym that I could find. FR-1…FR-10, UJ-1…UJ-4, SM-1…SM-3, and SM-C1…SM-C2 are each contiguous and unique with no gaps or duplicates. Traceability closes in both directions: every FR carries a "Realizes UJ-n" and every UJ is realized by at least one FR (UJ-1→FR-1,2,3,4,10; UJ-2→FR-8,10; UJ-3→FR-6,9; UJ-4→FR-5,7), so there are no floating UJs and no orphan FRs. Both primary SMs name the FR they validate and both counter-metrics name what they counterbalance — a loop most PRDs skip entirely. Cross-references use section numbers and Glossary terms, not "see above," and each §4 subsection survives being pulled out alone.

What breaks is the brownfield layer, and it breaks in exactly the places the architecture workflow reads first.

The most serious is a conflation of two separate existing mechanisms. The addendum states: "`ai/chat.rs` already parses a fenced ` ```tool_call ` block out of the model's response and already has a closed, enforced `ACTION_TYPES` set (mirrored in `chatActions.ts`) for the confirm-then-persist pattern used by `create_expense` etc." Verified, these are two distinct pipelines in two different files. `ai/chat.rs` has `parse_tool_call` (line ~207) for a ` ```tool_call ` fence carrying **read-only queries** (`query_expenses`, `query_maintenance_status`). The closed write-action set lives in **`src-tauri/src/commands/chat.rs`** (`ACTION_TYPES`, line ~746: `create_expense`, `create_budget_category`, `batch_actions`, `update_balance`, `create_account`, `update_asset_value`), is parsed from a **different** ` ```action ` fence in `ChatMessageBubble.tsx`, and is mirrored by `CHAT_ACTION_TYPES` in `chatActions.ts`. There is no `ACTION_TYPES` in `ai/chat.rs` at all. §4.3's description inherits the merge — "parsed for a structured Home Surface description the same way today's chat parses a `tool_call` block, and a new entry in the existing closed action-type set backs persistence" — which proposes riding a read-query-shaped fence while registering in a write-action set. (§0's own file list is fine; it names the two things separately. The damage is in the addendum and §4.3.)

The second is the component catalog's cost. §0 frames the whole feature as building "directly on functionality that already exists and ships today" and asserts "No new AI subsystem is introduced," and the addendum claims CopilotKit's "any custom component your frontend already renders" maps to "Nixus's existing recharts-backed chart components." Verified: of FR-1's six Display Components, only **Table** (`packages/shared/src/ui/table.tsx`) and the **Metric** primitive (`Stat`/`SubStat` in `packages/shared/src/ui/stat.tsx`, composed into `DashboardMetricCard.tsx`) exist as reusable. **`PieChart` does not appear anywhere in `apps/desktop/src`** — zero occurrences; it must be built from scratch. And LineChart/BarChart/AreaChart exist only as three bespoke, domain-specific components (`NetWorthTrendChart`, `SpendingTrendChart`, `ProjectionChart`) — not generic series-agnostic primitives — so each needs extraction and generalization before a catalog can bind arbitrary Data Sources to them. §6.1's scope line "Six Display Components + four Layout Primitives (§4.1)" reads as catalog registration; it is actually one new chart type plus three extractions plus four new layout primitives.

### Findings

- **critical** `tool_call` and `ACTION_TYPES` are conflated into one mechanism (addendum "Tool-call extension vs. new parallel AI pathway"; §4.3 Description) — `ai/chat.rs`'s ` ```tool_call ` fence carries read-only queries; the closed write-action set is `ACTION_TYPES` in `commands/chat.rs:746` parsed from a separate ` ```action ` fence. The addendum attributes both to `ai/chat.rs`, and §4.3 proposes a design spanning them as if they were one pipeline. `bmad-architecture` consuming this starts from a wrong map of the code, and the inherited-for-free claim ("FR-7's graceful-degradation behavior is inherited for free rather than re-implemented") depends on which of the two paths is actually extended. *Fix:* correct the addendum to describe the two mechanisms separately with their real file locations and fence names, then restate §4.3 as a decision about which one the Home Surface flow extends — the ` ```action ` confirm-then-persist path is the closer analog for a persisted mutation, and choosing it has UX consequences (a confirmation card before a Widget appears) the PRD currently doesn't contemplate.
- **high** The component-reuse premise is materially overstated (§0 ¶2, §4.1 FR-1, §6.1 bullet 3; addendum "Prior art") — `PieChart` exists nowhere in the codebase, and Line/Bar/Area exist only as bespoke domain components, not generic primitives; only Table and the Stat-based Metric card are genuinely reusable today. "Builds directly on functionality that already exists and ships today" sets an effort expectation the catalog cannot meet. *Fix:* in §4.1, split the catalog into components that exist as reusable primitives, components needing extraction from existing domain charts (naming them), and components to be built new (`PieChart`) — then adjust §6.1's scope line so story creation sizes this correctly.
- **medium** "Mirroring the existing static dashboard's domains" is inaccurate in both directions (§4.2 Description, §6.1 bullet 4) — Verified against `routes/index.tsx`: account balances are *not* on `/` (only a link to `/wealth/accounts`), and assets appear only folded inside a net-worth card — while `FinancialHealthCard`, `SavingsProjectsCard`, and `YearToDateCard` *are* on `/` and are absent from the seven. The stated rationale ("so the AI has meaningful material to work with from day one") rests on a mapping that doesn't hold. *Fix:* either justify the seven Data Sources on their own merits, or correct the claim and say which dashboard domains were deliberately excluded.
- **medium** Existing codified IA constraints go unacknowledged (§4.5) — The nav claim is right on names and order, but `lib/navigation.ts` enforces a hard cap of four Finance destinations via a `FourDestinations` type and an explicit "no fifth destination" rule, and Car and AI each have their own second-level segmented nav. This codebase has deliberate, encoded IA conventions; a new top-level rail item lands beside them with no mention that the convention exists. *Fix:* note the two-level rail/destination structure and the four-destination cap in §4.5 so the architecture phase knows whether Home is a fourth rail item, a destination, or something that changes the convention.

## Shape fit — adequate

Calibrated as the user specified — solo-built, pre-alpha, brownfield, chain-top — the shape is largely right, and in a few places deliberately and correctly restrained. There are no personas, which is the correct call for a single-profile app with one archetype; §2.1's JTBD carries the same load at a fraction of the ceremony. There is no rollout plan, no stakeholder matrix, no competitive analysis, and no compliance traceability — all appropriately absent. Rigor is concentrated where it pays: FR consequences, Glossary, Non-Goals. Brownfield handling is mostly right in form (§0 names the specific files, FR-10 explicitly protects existing behavior, §5/§6.2 distinguish new from existing), even where the specific references are wrong — the *discipline* of grounding claims in named code is there.

The four UJs are load-bearing rather than overhead here, which is the right judgment: this is a consumer-facing surface whose core interaction is multi-turn conversation, so walking the flows adds real information. UJ-4 ("Malformed generation self-heals") is a system-behavior journey rather than a user-goal one, slightly off-shape, but it earns its slot because the self-healing is user-visible and it's the only place the failure experience gets described end to end.

The one section forced into the wrong shape is Success Metrics. §7 is structured for a product with an analytics pipeline — three tiers, percentage targets, a session-scoped behavioral metric — on a local-first desktop app whose README states that nothing leaves the machine except opt-in AI requests and opt-in sync, and which describes no telemetry anywhere. SM-3 ("% of users who, having built one Widget, build a second one... within the same session") requires cross-user session analytics that do not exist and whose introduction would cut against the product's stated privacy posture. SM-2's first-attempt validation pass rate is the one genuinely worth having, and it's also the one most obviously requiring instrumentation. The PRD never says how any SM will be collected — and on this product that isn't an oversight of detail, it's a collision between two of the document's own commitments. Worth noting too that the stated "launch-grade" calibration sits against `status: draft` on a product whose README names its June 2026 north star as "learn whether anyone besides the builder would actually use it — not growth, not revenue"; the metrics are shaped for the former and the product is in the latter.

### Findings

- **high** Success Metrics assume instrumentation the product doesn't have and arguably shouldn't (§7, all of it) — No SM names a collection mechanism; SM-3 requires cross-user session analytics on a local-first app with no telemetry, which collides with the privacy stance §1 and §4.2 lean on. *Fix:* state the measurement path per SM. The honest shape for a pre-alpha is probably: SM-2 as a locally-logged, user-visible-or-opt-in-reported counter (or a dogfooding/eval-harness number measured by the builder against a fixed prompt set rather than in production), SM-1 as a test assertion, SM-3 as a qualitative beta-feedback question rather than a percentage. Add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` naming the telemetry-vs-privacy tension rather than leaving it implicit.
- **low** UJ protagonists are unnamed — correct for this shape, flagged so downstream doesn't "fix" it (§2.3) — All four UJs open with "A user" / "A privacy-conscious Nixus user" rather than a named protagonist. For a single-user, single-profile, solo-built app, naming an invented persona would manufacture exactly the theater this rubric penalizes elsewhere. *Fix:* no change to the UJs; add a one-line note in §0 or §2.3 stating that protagonists are intentionally generic because the product has a single user archetype, so a later reviewer or workflow doesn't read the absence as an omission.

## Mechanical notes
